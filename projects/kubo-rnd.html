03 — R&D · greenhouse technology

Analyse, build, test: R&D at KUBO

An internship and part-time job in the R&D department of greenhouse builder KUBO. No fixed project, just real problems: an airflow test on an 85 m hose, a tool I redesigned and took to production, and a prototype built only to understand a risk.

ContextInternship → part-time
WhereKUBO Group, Monster
Year2025
TeamR&D department

How much air comes out?

01
Test setup · data

KUBO's Ultra Clima system uses an Air Handling Unit (AHU) per path in the greenhouse: a heating and cooling block with a fan that blows air through a long hose. Growers want to know how much air goes in, because that sets how quickly the air in the greenhouse is replaced.

This was the biggest project of my internship. We built a test setup around one AHU and an 85 m hose, and ran the fan at different speeds. Airflow can be worked out in more than one way, so we measured several things at once to compare them.

Even with a detailed test plan we kept running into surprises, and had to adjust the setup and redo parts of the test. Testing takes longer than you plan, and that's the point of it.

My role
  • Building the setup
  • Running the tests
  • Processing the data
  • Improving the setup

A tool, from lost files to steel

02
Redesign → production

A greenhouse near the coast in Portugal had a problem: salty air made the aluminium corrode, the corrosion built up pressure, and the glass panes started to break. The temporary fix was to create more room in the frame around the glass, using a special tool that pulls part of the profile up when you tighten a bolt.

One tool existed. Three more were needed, but the 3D models were gone and the people who made the original had left. What started as "can you get some quotes?" became a project I mostly ran on my own.

My role
  • 3D modelling
  • Redesign
  • Prototyping & testing
  • Quotes & production
  • Contact with manufacturers

A prototype to understand a risk

03
Research · heat test

For a project in Saudi Arabia, another department asked whether the PVC and aluminium parts would cause problems in that climate. In a brainstorm we named three risks: UV, high temperatures and thermal expansion.

With a colleague I took on heat. We made a small test setup with the PVC strips that hold the glass, put it in an oven at different temperatures, and looked at flexibility and deformation afterwards. The result: at high temperatures the PVC quickly loses strength and shape, so the company knew early to look for alternatives.

For me this was new: building a prototype not to test a solution, but to understand the problem.

My role
  • Brainstorm
  • Test plan
  • Running the test
  • Reporting

What I took from it

04

  • Plan for the surprises

    The airflow test needed more rounds than planned. You test because you can't predict everything, so I now build that time in from the start.

  • Own the whole route

    The tool only mattered once it was made, tested and sent out. Modelling it was the easy part; getting it made and working was the job.

  • Prototype early, and cheaply

    A 3D print showed whether the tool worked before anyone paid for stainless steel. And a quick oven test showed a risk before it became a problem.
