Hearing motor faults without opening the motor

E-bike motors get thrown away when they could be repaired. I built a tool that listens to the vibrations of a running motor to tell a healthy one from a broken one, from the outside.

The problem

01
Replace, not repair

When an e-bike motor fails, it usually gets replaced as a whole. Yet most failures are mechanical: worn bearings and gears that can technically be repaired.

The bike mechanic is the first to see a struggling motor, but can't look inside. Diagnostic software only reads error codes. Experts at refurbishment companies diagnose by ear and feel: "this sounds wrong." So motors reach them too late, or not at all.

Inside the motor

02
9 motors, 6 brands

I took nine mid-drive motors apart and mapped every part and connection. Despite the different brands, they all share the same basic architecture. Combined with interviews, this showed where they fail.

The prototype

03
Piezo + microcontroller

A piezo sensor is pressed against the motor housing by a 3D-printed 'finger' on a heavy, rigid stand. The motor runs in walk-assist mode, held on by a clamp, so nobody touches the bike.

A Seeeduino XIAO samples the signal at ~4000 samples per second for 10 seconds. Python scripts plot it live, then calculate RMS, an FFT and the energy per frequency band.

    What I found

    05

    • Broken motors vibrate more

      In almost every case the defective motor had a higher RMS. With all bearings damaged, it was nearly four times the healthy level. RMS works as a quick first check.

    • Defects shift the energy

      Every damaged Bosch motor moved energy into the 80–160 Hz band, and most defects left a slightly different pattern. That hints at telling defects apart, not just spotting them.

    • The raw signal matters

      The broken gear produced a peak once per crank rotation that RMS and band energy completely missed. A good tool needs to look at the time signal too.

    • Proof of principle, not a product

      Two motor models, two measurements per case. It shows the method works; reliable thresholds per defect need much more data.

    The concept

    06
    A tool for the workshop

    A wireless sensor on a stand plus software with a database of healthy baselines per motor type. The mechanic measures during a normal service, in about fifteen minutes, and gets advice on what to do next.

    1. 01Prepare

      Hang the bike, remove chain and covers.

    2. 02Position

      Rest the motor against the sensor finger.

    3. 03Run

      Clamp the walk-assist button.

    4. 04Measure

      Start recording from the laptop.

    5. 05Decide

      Compare with the baseline and get advice.
